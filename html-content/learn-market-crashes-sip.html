Why Market Crashes Are Good for Your SIP

When the stock market crashes, most investors panic. They stop SIPs, watch the news in horror, and sometimes sell everything at a loss. What they don't realise: a market crash is the single best thing that can happen to a disciplined SIP investor. Here's exactly why — with real numbers from March 2020.

Understanding Rupee Cost Averaging

Rupee Cost Averaging (RCA) is the mathematical magic behind why SIPs are so powerful during volatile markets. When you invest a fixed amount every month, you automatically buy more units when prices are low and fewer units when prices are high. Over time, your average cost per unit ends up lower than the average market price over the same period.

Think of it this way: if samosas cost ₹10 each today and you buy 10, you get 10 samosas. Next month they cost ₹5 each and you again spend ₹100 — you get 20 samosas. Your average cost is ₹6.67 per samosa, not ₹7.50 (the simple average of ₹10 and ₹5). A market crash is like the samosa shop having a 50% off sale. Your SIP buys double the units.

The March 2020 gift: An investor running a ₹5,000/month SIP in Nifty 50 index fund from January 2019 — who continued without pause through the crash — ended up with approximately 23% more corpus by December 2020 compared to someone who paused SIPs from February to June 2020. Five months of pausing cost them years of compounding.

The COVID Crash in Numbers: March 2020

In January 2020, the Nifty 50 was trading near 12,300. By March 23, 2020, it had collapsed to 7,610 — a fall of 38% in just 40 trading days. Panic was everywhere. News headlines screamed "worst crash since 2008." Most retail investors stopped or paused their SIPs.

But here's what was actually happening: a ₹5,000 SIP that would have bought approximately 2.7 units at Nifty 50 Fund NAV of ₹185 in January, was suddenly buying 4.7 units at NAV of ₹107 in March. Every single rupee of that SIP instalment was acquiring 74% more ownership of India's top 50 companies. Wealth was being transferred — from the panicking sellers to the calm, systematic investors.

The Emotional Investor vs. The Rational Investor

Two investors, Amit and Priya, both had ₹10,000/month SIPs in the same Nifty 50 index fund. Amit panicked in March 2020 and paused his SIP for 6 months. Priya kept investing without a second thought.

By December 2021, Amit's corpus was ₹4.8 lakh. Priya's corpus was ₹5.9 lakh — 23% more. The difference? Priya bought heavily discounted units for 6 months while Amit sat on cash. Those units doubled in value as markets recovered. Amit lost ₹1.1 lakh in potential gains by trying to "wait for the bottom." Priya didn't need to predict the bottom. The SIP did it automatically.

Historical Evidence: Markets Always Recover

India's Nifty 50 has experienced several major crashes since its inception in 1996. In every single instance, the market has not only recovered but reached new all-time highs:

  • 2000-02 Dot-com crash: -55% drawdown. Recovered fully within 3 years.
  • 2008 Global Financial Crisis: -60% drawdown. Recovered fully within 2 years.
  • 2011 Eurozone crisis: -28% drawdown. Recovered within 18 months.
  • 2020 COVID crash: -38% drawdown. Recovered in 8 months — fastest ever.

The average recovery time for Nifty 50 from a 30%+ crash is approximately 18 months. If your SIP horizon is 10+ years, not a single crash in India's market history would have resulted in a loss. Every crash was an opportunity in disguise.

What You Should Actually Do During a Crash

The best action during a market crash is the hardest emotionally: do nothing. Keep your SIP running. If you have surplus cash, consider increasing your SIP amount or making a lump sum purchase. This is called "buying the dip" — and it's the strategy that creates genuine wealth over the long term. Pausing or stopping your SIP during a crash is mathematically equivalent to closing your shop during the busiest sale season.

Key Takeaway

Market crashes are a SIP investor's best friend. Rupee cost averaging means your fixed monthly investment buys significantly more units during a crash. Since Indian markets have recovered from every crash in history, those extra units become enormous wealth when markets rebound.
