LTCG vs STCG: When Should You Redeem?

The difference between a 10% tax and a 15% tax might sound trivial until you apply it to ₹5 lakh in gains. Suddenly that's ₹25,000 real money staying in your wallet just by waiting a few extra months. Understanding LTCG and STCG is one of the simplest — and most underused — ways to legally optimise your investment returns.

What are Capital Gains?

Capital gains are the profits you earn when you sell a mutual fund at a higher price than you bought it. If you invested ₹1 lakh in a fund and its value grew to ₹1.5 lakh when you redeem, your capital gain is ₹50,000. This ₹50,000 is subject to capital gains tax. The rate depends on how long you held the investment before selling — this is where STCG and LTCG come in.

STCG: Short-Term Capital Gains (Less Than 12 Months)

If you sell equity mutual fund units that you've held for less than 12 months, any profit is classified as Short-Term Capital Gain (STCG). The tax rate on STCG from equity funds is a flat 15% — regardless of your income tax slab. So if you invest ₹5 lakh and it grows to ₹6 lakh in 8 months, your ₹1 lakh gain is taxed at 15% = ₹15,000 tax.

LTCG: Long-Term Capital Gains (More Than 12 Months)

If you hold equity mutual fund units for more than 12 months before selling, profits are classified as Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG). The tax rate is 10%, and crucially, the first ₹1 lakh of LTCG per financial year is completely exempt from tax. So on a ₹5 lakh gain held for 14 months: (₹5L − ₹1L exemption) × 10% = ₹40,000 tax. Versus STCG: ₹5L × 15% = ₹75,000. The difference is ₹35,000 — simply by waiting two more months to sell.

The 12-month rule in practice: Before redeeming, check your fund's purchase date. If you're 9 or 10 months into holding, waiting 2–3 more months to cross the 12-month mark can save you 5% in tax on the entire gain. That's often thousands of rupees for doing nothing except being patient.

Tax Harvesting: The Advanced Strategy

Tax harvesting is the practice of strategically redeeming up to ₹1 lakh in LTCG gains every financial year before March 31, and immediately re-investing the same amount. This "resets" your cost basis to the current NAV, while you pocket the ₹1 lakh exempt gain completely tax-free. Over 10–15 years of regular tax harvesting, this can save ₹8–12 lakh in LTCG taxes that would otherwise accumulate and become payable in a large lump at the end.

Debt Fund Taxation (Post-2023 Rule Change)

Important update: SEBI changed debt fund taxation in April 2023. Previously, debt funds held for 3+ years qualified for LTCG at 20% with indexation benefit. Now, all debt fund gains are taxed at your income tax slab rate regardless of holding period — the same as FD interest. This makes debt funds less tax-efficient for long-term investors in high tax brackets, and shifts the preference towards equity funds for wealth-building goals.

When Should You Actually Redeem?

The answer is goal-based, not market-based. Redeem when you need the money for the goal you invested for — a house down payment, child's education, retirement. Never redeem just because markets are at a high or you're nervous. But when you do need to redeem: check whether you're past the 12-month mark. If you're close, waiting a little longer is often worth it. And remember to redeem old units first (FIFO — First In, First Out), as older units are more likely to qualify for LTCG treatment.

Key Takeaway

Hold equity funds for 12+ months to qualify for LTCG tax at 10% instead of STCG at 15%. The first ₹1 lakh of LTCG per year is tax-free. Strategically harvesting this exemption annually can save lakhs over a long investment horizon.
